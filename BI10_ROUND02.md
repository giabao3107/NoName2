# Business Intelligence Season 10 — Round 02: Synthesize

**Event:** Business Intelligence Season 10, Round 02 — _Synthesize_

## Business Context

A financial services company has access to customer profiles, historical financial behavior, leads, contracts, and call information.

However, the company does not have a unified view of:

- How financially healthy each customer is.
- How engaged each customer is.
- Which customers have high future potential.
- Which customers may be financially stressed.
- What action should be taken for each customer.

> **Your team has 30 hours to turn raw customer data into an actionable intelligence solution.**

## Challenge 1: Customer Financial Health Score

1. Analyze the factors that may reflect customers’ financial health.
2. Develop a Customer Financial Health Score ranging from 0–100, or an equivalent scoring methodology.
3. Segment customers into meaningful business groups.
4. Identify the key factors that have positive or negative impacts on financial health.
5. Explain and provide evidence to justify why the team’s scoring methodology is reasonable and appropriate.

## Challenge 2: Customer Engagement Score

1. Identify the key factors that reflect customer engagement.
2. Analyze customer behavior based on:
   - Recency
   - Frequency
   - Interaction behavior
   - Lead activity
   - Product interaction
3. Develop a Customer Engagement Score.
4. Segment customers based on their level of engagement.
5. Identify the key factors that drive or reduce customer engagement.
6. Validate whether the Engagement Score is associated with business outcomes.

## Challenge 3: Customer Segmentation

1. Combine Financial Health and Engagement.
2. Identify customer opportunities.
3. Recommend the Next Best Action.
4. Explain why this action is appropriate.

## Important Notes

1. Pay close attention to data quality and relationships between datasets.
2. Machine Learning is not mandatory. A complex model is not necessarily a better solution.
3. The scoring methodology must be explainable.
4. Do not confuse correlation with causation.
5. Recommendations must be actionable.
6. Avoid using demographic factors to determine risk without sufficient evidence or justification.
